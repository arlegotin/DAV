### Post-pilot screening limitation

A later review of the result found that the implemented sequence screen enumerated four-check orders, with non-PASS stopping, but omitted shorter sequences. It therefore cannot establish that all useful acquisition strategies lack headroom. A CPU-only, outcome-informed diagnostic of T then R2 accepted **three correct and one incorrect** proposal, deferring 20, versus T alone accepting three correct and three incorrect. Its mean loss was **8.14**, below T alone's 9.09 but above always defer's 6.80. This preserves a possible combined-verification benefit without establishing adaptive or dependency-aware value. R1's constant FAIL output still provides no observed ability to distinguish correct proposals.

This diagnostic was chosen after inspecting the pilot, acquired no new evidence, and leaves the original gate, policies and records unchanged. [Diagnostic records](../runs/m2/post-pilot-short-sequence-diagnostic.json). The five-correct threshold is a declared screening choice, rather than a theorem about whether dependence-aware verification can work. The broader idea remains untested; prospective diagnostic or pilot revisions must retain this result and freeze their own choices before new outcomes.
